Backtest · NSE equity · 2011–2026

The Varsity crossover, tested

Zerodha Varsity’s Build a Trading Algo with AI teaches you to build a Nifty 100 SMA(6)/SMA(30) crossover scanner without writing code. It builds it well. It never asks whether the signal makes money. Here is that question, answered over fifteen and a half years with fills at the next open, every Zerodha charge, and a universe that does not know the future.

Diagnosis

The universe was fine. The rule was the problem.

Buying the same hundred stocks the strategy was choosing from — equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly, no timing whatsoever, paying exactly the same charges — returned 13.65% a year. That beats the index. So the stock pool was not the issue.

Bolting the crossover rule on top of that pool took it from 13.65% down to 1.92%. The timing rule destroyed 11.74 points a year relative to simply holding what it was already looking at.

The decisive test

The right null for a timing rule is not zero and not buy-and-hold. It is random timing at the same exposure: keep the strategy’s own 1,574 trades and its exact holding-period distribution, but draw the entry dates and the stocks at random from the same universe. Over 1,000 bootstrap runs the strategy averaged +0.284% per trade against the null’s +0.461% — it sits at the 28th percentile. Throwing darts would have done better.

A second bootstrap separates the two ways a rule can be wrong. Holding the strategy’s own entry days fixed and randomising only which stock it bought, the null still won: +0.425% against the strategy’s +0.284%, putting it at the 29th percentile. So it is not that the crossover picks reasonable stocks on bad days, or good days and bad stocks. Neither half carries information.

Sensitivity

No assumption rescues it

The result is not an artefact of a pessimistic slippage number. NSE publishes a median impact cost of about 2 bps for a ₹1 lakh Nifty 100 order — but that is measured inside the continuous session, and it only applies to you if your order is provably inside the pre-open call auction. Measured first-minute moves on these names run a median of about 21 bps.

Overfitting

Tuning helps — but not the way it looks

The 6/30 pair was chosen on camera, out of the air. The moment you try several pairs and keep the best, the number you report is no longer one you could have earned. So: run all 41 pairs, then walk forward — three years to train, one year to trade, the pair re-chosen each January on training-period Sharpe alone.

Honest tuning does lift the rule from 3.3% to 11.2%, and it earns a genuinely shallower drawdown than the index (−27% against −38%). But it still finishes below the index on both return and Sharpe. And look at what it selects: in twelve of thirteen years, the training window chose a long moving average of 100 or 200 days. Never anything close to 30.

What the parameter grid is really measuring

Across all 41 pairs, the correlation between CAGR and the log of trade count is −0.86. The six best pairs all use a 200-day long average and trade 269–438 times; the five worst use a 20–30 day average and trade 1,900–2,200 times. The surface is not measuring signal quality. It is measuring how successfully each pair avoided trading. The video’s 6/30 ranks 33rd of 41, and only 2 of the 41 beat the index.

Method

How the no-leak claim is enforced

“No data leakage” is a claim, and claims need tests. The engine processes each day in a fixed order — fill yesterday’s queued orders at today’s open, credit dividends, mark the book to today’s close, and only then read signals and queue tomorrow’s orders. A decision made on day t cannot be filled before the open of t+1. Three independent tests try to break that.

01

Truncation

Signals computed on data[:t] must equal signals computed on the full sample and then sliced to t. If anything peeked ahead, cutting the input short would change the answer.

02

Future scramble

Replace every bar after date T with noise — prices multiplied by random factors between 0.4 and 2.5 — and re-run. The equity curve up to T must be bit-identical. This is the strongest of the three because it catches leakage through channels the author never thought about.

03

Fill timing

Every entry price must equal the next session’s open moved by exactly the slippage assumption, and the crossover that caused it must have fired strictly earlier. No trade may fill on the bar that produced its own signal.

Plus: the point-in-time universe is itself causal under truncation; turning costs off must improve results, or the cost model is not really wired into the P&L; and the fast sparse-crossover index must produce trade-for-trade identical output to a dense scan of every column. Fifteen tests, all passing.

Costs

What a round trip actually costs

Zerodha charges nothing for delivery brokerage, which is exactly why the real cost is easy to overlook. STT alone is 20 bps of a round trip, and it is the largest single line by a wide margin.

Setup

Running it on your Zerodha account

The video installs Codex, Python, Node, React, Vite and FastAPI, then pays ₹500 a month for a Kite Connect app. For a daily-timeframe scanner none of that is load-bearing. The Kite MCP server is already wired into this project and needs no subscription; end-of-day prices are free.

The loop stays semi-automated, which is what the video recommends and what the results argue for even harder. Claude produces the table and the order sheet; you decide.

The bearish half is not a trade

Worth being blunt about, because the video presents bullish and bearish signals symmetrically. A retail account cannot hold a short equity position overnight in India. SEBI bans naked short selling and every seller must honour delivery at T+1 settlement; Zerodha rejects a CNC sell without holdings, and an intraday short is force-closed the same session. A bearish crossover is an exit signal for something you already own. Half the scanner’s output is not actionable as shown.

Caveats

What is still wrong with this backtest

Stated so you can discount the result yourself — and note that every one of these points the same way, against the strategy.

  • Residual survivorship bias. The point-in-time universe is drawn from symbols that are listed today. Companies delisted or merged away over fifteen years are absent, and they were disproportionately the losers. The true number is worse than 1.92%, not better.
  • Free data. Prices come from Yahoo, not NSE bhavcopy, and Yahoo revises history, so re-pulling the same window will move these figures. How much has never been measured, so no error bar is quoted here — an earlier draft claimed ±0.5 points and that number traced to nothing on disk. The 8.78-point shortfall is far larger than any plausible revision.
  • Fills at the printed open. NSE’s daily open is the pre-open call auction price, and a retail market order is not guaranteed to be inside that auction. The 25 bps assumption stands in for that uncertainty.
  • Circuit bands are approximated by a flat 10% gap test rather than the real per-scrip dynamic band, ASM/GSM surveillance state and series restrictions. Only 2 fills were blocked by it; the real number would be somewhat higher.
  • No taxes. Short-term capital gains would take a further bite out of a strategy with a 25-day median holding period, and would not touch a buy-and-hold benchmark.
Verdict

What to actually do with this

The video is a good tutorial about tooling and a bad tutorial about evidence. Everything it teaches — wire up the broker API, describe the rule in English, get a ranked table — works, and works better than it shows, because you do not need the ₹500 app or the React front end. The gap is that it stops at the moment a table appears on screen, which is precisely the moment the interesting question starts.

Keep the scanner. It is a genuinely useful lens on what is moving. Do not trade its output as a system: on this evidence a 6/30 crossover on the Nifty 100 is a mechanism for converting 24 bps of charges and 50 bps of slippage, 1,574 times, into an eight-point annual shortfall against an index fund you could have bought once.

If you want to keep pulling this thread, the useful direction is not a different moving-average pair — the grid says that surface is measuring turnover, not skill. It is a signal with a real economic reason to work, tested with this same machinery, against these same nulls. The machinery is the part worth keeping.
